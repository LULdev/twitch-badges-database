Members join a pool for a specific badge and hour; the site reminds everyone at the time and, after the next potat sync, publishes the measured owner-count step for that badge during the pool hour plus the pool's contribution ('137 owners in one hour'). The deliverable is evidence — a coordinated claim becomes a recorded catalog event with a chart, not just an RSVP list. Pool pages keep the badge's window, the participant count and the resulting chart as a small public record. Effort: M. Risk if built badly: the pool claims credit for organic claims landing in the same hour, so the measurement must compare against the badge's own rolling hourly baseline rather than against zero. Why: It is the one social mechanic only this site can offer: a group action that visibly moves a number in the catalog. Source: new-ideas-07-profile.json

Invite Lattice Tree

Details

Members invite others and both ends of the invite are recorded, producing a visible generation tree that shows how the community grew; an invitee starts with a small welcome grant and the inviter earns a purely cosmetic mark for each generation that stays active. The lattice is browsable ('see my branch'), every node is a normal profile, and an invite cannot be reattributed after the fact. Effort: M. Risk if built badly: it creates a status hierarchy and invite farming, so grants are one per verified Twitch account and the tree must never display anything a profile keeps private. Why: Growth has a shape, and letting members see their own branch makes the community feel like something with a history. Source: new-ideas-07-profile.json

Streamer Channel Link

Details

A member who streams can link their Twitch channel to their profile, which then shows a cached live-or-offline pill, a pinned note they write for their viewers, and the badges currently claimable so a viewer can act while watching. The pill resolves through a five-minute cache — one Helix stream lookup per channel per window, well inside the existing hourly budget — and linking requires re-authenticating with Twitch so nobody can claim someone else's channel. The link is part of the public profile, not a separate widget page. Effort: M. Risk if built badly: live lookups blow past the rate budget on popular channels, so the cache window and a per-channel lock are the guard. Why: Streamers and their viewers are already the site's audience, and the profile is where that relationship should resolve. Source: new-ideas-07-profile.json

Vouch Chain Provenance

Details

An established member can vouch for a newer one, and the vouch appears on the newcomer's profile as a chain ('vouched by @a, who was vouched by @b'). A vouch unlocks a small set of social surfaces — guestbook stamps, relay hops, race entry — for accounts too new to qualify on their own; vouches are one-way, attributed, revocable with a reason, and an established member may hold only a few live at once. Effort: M. Risk if built badly: vouch farming as a growth hack, so the small per-voucher limit plus revocation and the visible chain keep it costly. Why: New members cannot use the social layer until someone trusts them, and a visible chain makes that trust a real commitment. Source: new-ideas-07-profile.json

Commitment Reliability Record

Details

A profile-facing log of the social commitments a member accepted and completed — races finished, relays continued, review windows honoured, introductions taken up — with abandoned ones listed too. It is a plain dated list with outcomes, deliberately not a single score, and it gates nothing: visibility is the only consequence. The rows come from the commitment features themselves, so it is a read model rather than new tracking. Effort: S. Risk if built badly: it becomes a shame wall punishing one busy week, so it lists a bounded recent window and never reduces to a number. Why: On a site where members now make each other promises, keeping a record of who follows through is the cheapest fairness available. Source: new-ideas-07-profile.json

Annual Community Awards

Details

Once a year members nominate and vote in a small fixed set of categories — most helpful, best curator, best data catch, friendliest newcomer — and winners carry a dated award line on their profile permanently, with the nomination text quoted. The ballot is capped at one nomination per category per member, votes are per account, and results publish only after a reveal date so interim tallies cannot be watched. It is the community rewarding behaviour rather than data ranking collectors. Effort: M. Risk if built badly: coordinated voting and winner cliques, so a minimum account age to vote and unpublished interim counts are the guard. Why: It is the one annual ritual that recognises the member behaviour the leaderboards are blind to. Source: new-ideas-07-profile.json

Open House Windows

Details

A member can schedule a window — announced up to a week ahead, lasting 24 or 48 hours — during which their inventory is public and then closes itself; the profile shows a countdown and a visitor counter that resets each time. Because it is time-based rather than a standing preference, a member can show off for a weekend without going permanently public, and visitors see a normal collection during the window and the usual notice after it. Effort: M. Risk if built badly: a forgotten window leaves an inventory public indefinitely, so closure must be enforced at query time from the timestamp, never by a cron that can fail. Why: It gives private collectors a safe, bounded way to be seen, which the current binary setting cannot. Source: new-ideas-07-profile.json

Per-Panel Audience Scopes

Details

Each profile panel — inventory, trajectory chart, followers, guestbook, review digest — gets its own audience instead of one global flag: everyone, signed-in members, my crew, or private. The audience vocabulary is small and fixed, one shared resolver serves every read path, and a hidden panel renders a boundary notice rather than collapsing the page. Effort: M. Risk if built badly: a panel scoped to crew is read by a query that never joins crew membership and leaks, so the resolver has to be on every read path, not most of them. Why: Members want to show their inventory to their crew and their trajectory to everyone, and a single switch cannot express that. Source: new-ideas-07-profile.json

Ephemeral Social Traces

Details

A member sets a retention for their own social output — guestbook stamps they leave, admiration marks, relay notes, review justifications — choosing 7, 30 or 90 days, after which the row is deleted and the trace disappears from everyone's view. A page lists exactly what is scheduled to vanish and when. Effort: M. Risk if built badly: deleting a trace breaks another member's chain or reply, so removals must degrade gracefully (a hop becomes 'a retired member') instead of breaking the artifact. Why: It gives members control over their own trail without forcing the site into a full moderation-and-deletion project. Source: new-ideas-07-profile.json
