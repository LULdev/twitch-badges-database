A public roadmap page rendered from the site's own ideas board: each item with its title, a short gist, its status (planned, in progress, shipped, declined) and a vote count members can raise once per item. Items that ship link to the changelog entry that shipped them, closing the loop between the promise and the release note, and the page gives the community a reason to return and gives their suggestions somewhere to land. Data: the ideas rows and statuses, the changelog for shipped links, per-member votes. Effort: M. Risk if built badly: it exposes internal items never meant to be public or reads as a commitment the team cannot keep; default every idea to private, require an explicit publish flag, and word the statuses as intent rather than promise. Why: The site has 229 researched ideas nobody outside the team can see, and publishing them converts an internal backlog into community trust. Source: new-ideas-02-content.json

Collector spotlight interviews

Details

A recurring interview with a notable collector, driven by their own data: the writer pulls the subject's headline numbers (rarest badge, completion percentage, longest-held badge) and builds the question set around them, then publishes the piece with an author byline, the subject's public profile link and their showcase badges rendered live. Subjects are recruited from the leaderboards and from members who opt in to being approached, and every badge mentioned links to its page. Data: the member's public profile, inventory aggregates, leaderboard position, an explicit opt-in flag. Effort: M. Risk if built badly: it interviews people who never consented or publishes numbers they consider private; require opt-in, let the subject review before publishing, and treat a private inventory as a decline. Why: Collectors are the audience and the subject matter, and a profile written from real collection data is content no directory can generate. Source: new-ideas-02-content.json

Return-position contract for the catalog

Details

Leaving the catalog for a badge and coming back loses the visitor's place: the grid re-renders from the top and the tile they opened is unmarked. Define a return-position contract that persists the scroll offset and the focused tile id per route in sessionStorage, restores them on back navigation, and paints a brief you-were-here ring on the tile that was opened, cleared on the next interaction. The state is client-only and survives the App Router remount. Effort: S. Risk if built badly: restoring a stale offset after the grid contents changed, dropping the visitor into the middle of a different list or onto a tile that no longer exists. Why: Catalog browsing is inherently exploratory, and punishing every dive into a badge with a lost position is a tax on the main activity. Source: new-ideas-03-design.json

Design Neutral

FLIP choreography for badges that arrive live

Details

The catalog re-renders when the sync adds badges, but new tiles appear by reflowing the grid and yanking whatever the visitor is reading. Give the grid a defined insert behaviour: new tiles animate in on entry while existing tiles FLIP to their new positions, and a compact N new badges pill appears above the grid instead of auto-scrolling, so the visitor chooses when to jump. Positions come from getBoundingClientRect before and after the render, gated behind the motion preference and capped so a bulk sync does not animate a hundred tiles. Effort: M. Risk if built badly: an animation on every revalidation that makes a stable grid feel unstable, or measurement work that forces a layout thrash on a large page. Why: This is a real-time database, so the grid changes under people's hands and that should feel like an update rather than a glitch. Source: new-ideas-03-design.json

Design Neutral

Coin-flight arcs from payout to HUD

Details

When a game, the wheel or the daily login pays out, the HUD balance simply changes with no link between cause and effect. Choreograph the payment instead: the result panel emits a short arc of coin glyphs that fly from the payout element to the HUD coin counter, which ticks up as they land, with the path computed from the two elements' bounding boxes and the glyph count capped so a jackpot does not spawn hundreds of elements. The motion preference and the motion-level setting skip the flight and update the number directly. Effort: S. Risk if built badly: dozens of animated nodes on a big win, an arc that keeps running after the visitor navigates away, or a displayed balance that drifts from the server value it animates toward. Why: The economy is the part of the site people interact with most, and the payout is the moment worth animating. Source: new-ideas-03-design.json
